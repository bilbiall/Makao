02

Why Kenya, why now

Kenya combines two things that rarely coexist in emerging rental markets: near-universal digital payments, and a rental sector that is almost entirely run on paper.

91%

Of Nairobi households are tenants, not owners — one of the highest urban rental rates in the world.

90%+

Mobile money penetration — Kenyans already pay rent by M-Pesa, they just do it with no digital record on the landlord's side.

77%

Of Kenyan rental stock is flats and apartments — multi-unit buildings that are painful to run from a notebook once portfolios grow.

Sources: Kenya National Bureau of Statistics population projections; Central Bank of Kenya / industry mobile-money penetration reporting, 2025; national housing survey data on urban tenure and rental stock composition. Figures are the most recent publicly reported estimates at time of writing and should be re-verified before external use in formal materials.

The gap Renty closes isn't payments — Kenyans already pay by M-Pesa. It's that nobody on the landlord's side can prove who paid, for what, or when.
03

The rollout sequence

Five phases, each with an exit condition before moving to the next. Early phases are deliberately small and hands-on — the goal is a repeatable playbook, not raw speed.

0
Months 1–3 · Nairobi beachhead

Prove the playbook with 10–20 landlords

Founder-led, concierge onboarding in one or two Nairobi neighborhoods. Every landlord is onboarded personally — the goal is to learn what breaks, not to scale yet.

  • Onboard 10–20 landlords, 150–300 units, across a mix of long-term and BnB
  • Every landlord live on M-Pesa STK Push and/or C2B within their first week
  • Weekly founder check-ins — collect friction points directly, ship fixes fast
  • Referral loop: happy landlords introduce the next cohort
Team
Founder only
Exit criteria
≥80% of onboarded landlords still active after 60 days; at least one unprompted referral
1
Months 4–6 · Nairobi scale-out

Turn the playbook into a repeatable motion

Expand across the wider Nairobi metro (5.8M+ people). First hires come in to remove the founder as the bottleneck.

  • Hire a Customer Success / Onboarding lead and a support agent
  • Ship a self-serve onboarding flow so not every landlord needs a personal walkthrough
  • Launch a caretaker/agent referral incentive (caretakers already have a role in the product)
  • First paid marketing test: Facebook/WhatsApp groups where Nairobi landlords already trade advice
Team
+2 (CS lead, support agent)
Exit criteria
100+ landlords, MRR covers CS + support headcount
2
Months 7–10 · Mombasa & Kisumu

First proof the model travels outside Nairobi

Coast (Mombasa, 1.5M+) and the lake region (Kisumu) — different tenant mix, different BnB seasonality, first real test of the platform outside the capital.

  • Hire one City Launch Lead per city — local, on the ground, commission-driven
  • Partner with local real-estate agent associations for warm introductions
  • Localize SMS templates and support hours to each city
  • Stand up city-level dashboards to compare unit economics against Nairobi
Team
+2 City Launch Leads
Exit criteria
Each city independently profitable on a per-landlord basis within 4 months of launch
3
Months 11–14 · The growth corridor

Nakuru, Eldoret, Thika, Nyeri

Fast-growing secondary towns along Kenya's main economic corridor. Smaller portfolios per landlord, higher volume of landlords — the playbook shifts toward volume, not white-glove onboarding.

  • Reuse the Mombasa/Kisumu playbook — no new product work required
  • Introduce a lightweight reseller/agent commission model for smaller towns
  • First dedicated backend hire to keep reliability high as landlord count grows
Team
+1 backend engineer, +reseller network
Exit criteria
Reseller-sourced landlords convert at ≥ the direct-sales rate
4
Months 15–24 · National coverage

Every county town, product-led

By now the product, support model, and reseller network should carry most new growth without a city-by-city launch team.

  • Self-serve signup is the default path for new landlords, everywhere
  • Reseller network covers remaining county towns
  • Evaluate the payments-take-rate revenue lever once volume justifies it (see the investor pitch)
Team
Stable core team + reseller network
Exit criteria
National footprint reached; shift focus to depth (more units per landlord) over breadth

05

Risks & how we handle them

Daraja / M-Pesa API dependency

Payments are the product's core value and run entirely through Safaricom's Daraja API. Mitigation: Pesapal already exists as a card/bank fallback; monitor Daraja status proactively.

SMS costs at scale

Bills, reminders and announcements all send SMS. Mitigation: email is a free second channel (already built), and SMS volume is directly tied to paying landlord count, so it scales with revenue.

Displacing an entrenched manual habit

Landlords have run rent books and WhatsApp groups for years. Mitigation: concierge onboarding in Phase 0 exists specifically to remove friction, not add it.

Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019

Tenant PII (ID numbers, payment history) is sensitive. Mitigation: manual, founder-reviewed verification instead of a third-party KYC vendor until volume justifies the cost and the compliance overhead that comes with it.

Informal competition

The real competitor isn't another SaaS tool — it's a WhatsApp group and a notebook. Mitigation: the product has to be strictly easier than doing nothing, which is why onboarding speed is an explicit Phase 0/1 metric.

WhatsApp integration, deliberately not built yet

Considered and explicitly deferred — official Meta Cloud API requires dedicating a business number exclusively, unofficial libraries are unreliable. Revisit once a city lead needs it operationally.
